import os

plays = pd.read_csv("data/clean/plays.csv")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.5))   # fig: the picture, ax: the chart in it

Call methods on ax to draw and label. figsize is width and height in inches.

The question firstSession 9

A chart is an answer to a question. Get the numbers with pandas, sorted, before drawing anything:

by_genre = (plays.groupby("genre")["minutes_played"]
            .mean()
            .sort_values(ascending=False))
print(by_genre.round(1).head(3))     # Jazz 6.6, Electronic 5.3, ...
top_genre, top_minutes = by_genre.index[0], by_genre.iloc[0]

Sorted bars make the ranking the first thing a reader sees.

Chart types

Bar: categoriesSession 9

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.5))
bars = ax.bar(by_genre.index, by_genre.values, color="#4f6ddb")

ax.set_title(f"{top_genre} plays last longest: {top_minutes:.1f} minutes on average")
ax.set_ylabel("average minutes per play")         # units on the axis
ax.bar_label(bars, fmt="{:.1f}")                  # the value on each bar
ax.spines[["top", "right"]].set_visible(False)    # borders that say nothing

fig.savefig("output/minutes_by_genre.png", dpi=150, bbox_inches="tight")
plt.close(fig)

The title states the answer: "Jazz plays last longest: 6.6 minutes on average", not "Minutes per genre".

Horizontal bar: long namesSession 9 Session 10

minutes = plays.groupby("artist_name")["minutes_played"].sum()
top = minutes.sort_values(ascending=False).head(8)
ranked = top.sort_values()                # barh draws from the bottom up

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.5))
bars = ax.barh(ranked.index, ranked.values, color="#4f6ddb")
ax.set_title(f"{ranked.index[-1]} and {ranked.index[-2]} are far ahead of the rest")
ax.set_xlabel("minutes played, 2024 to 2025")
ax.bar_label(bars, fmt="{:,.1f}", padding=3)      # padding: a gap after the bar
ax.spines[["top", "right"]].set_visible(False)
fig.savefig("output/top_artists.png", dpi=150, bbox_inches="tight")
plt.close(fig)
print(top.index[0])                       # Glass Tram, at the top of the chart

Or keep the biggest-first order and call ax.invert_yaxis(), as session 10's pipeline does.

Line: timeSession 9

per_month = plays.groupby(plays["played_at"].str[:7]).size()

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 4.5))
ax.plot(per_month.index, per_month.values, marker="o", color="#4f6ddb")
ax.set_title("Listening dips every summer, in both years")
ax.set_ylabel("plays per month")
ax.set_ylim(bottom=0)                       # so a dip is not a cliff
ax.tick_params(axis="x", labelrotation=90)  # 24 month labels, turned
ax.spines[["top", "right"]].set_visible(False)
fig.savefig("output/plays_per_month.png", dpi=150, bbox_inches="tight")
plt.close(fig)
print(per_month.idxmin(), per_month.min())  # 2025-08 57

A line says "this follows from that", which is only true when the x-axis has an order. Months, days, years: a line. Genres, devices, artists: bars.

Two charts side by sideSession 9

skip_rate = plays.groupby("device")["skipped"].mean().sort_values(ascending=False) * 100

fig, (left, right) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 4))   # 1 row, 2 charts
left.bar(skip_rate.index, skip_rate.values, color="#c0392b")
left.set_ylim(10, 14.5)
left.set_title("Zoomed in: speaker looks terrible")
right.bar(skip_rate.index, skip_rate.values, color="#4f6ddb")
right.set_ylim(0, 15)
right.set_title("From zero: they are all much the same")
right.bar_label(right.containers[0], fmt="{:.1f}%")
fig.savefig("output/skip_rate_two_ways.png", dpi=150, bbox_inches="tight")
plt.close(fig)
print(skip_rate.round(1).iloc[[0, -1]].to_dict())   # {'speaker': 14.0, 'car': 10.6}

Same numbers, two stories. Bars always start at zero: the length of a bar is the number.

Finishing

SavingSession 9

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.5))
ax.bar(by_genre.index, by_genre.values)
fig.savefig("output/chart.png", dpi=150, bbox_inches="tight")   # save FIRST
plt.close(fig)                                                  # then let it go
print(os.path.exists("output/chart.png"))                       # True

dpi=150 is sharp enough for a slide. bbox_inches="tight" stops long labels being cut off. In a script, plt.savefig() after plt.show() writes a blank picture. output/ is gitignored.

Checklist for every chartSession 9

  • Can I say the question in one sentence?
  • Does the title give the answer, not the topic?
  • Bars for categories, a line for time?
  • Do the bars start at zero?
  • Units on the axis, numbers rounded to what a person would say?
  • Would it make sense to someone who was not in the room?
