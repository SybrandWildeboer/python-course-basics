GROUP BY with several aggregates

groupby, agg, sort_values

Each line of agg is new_name=(column, what_to_do). Your session 4 homework, with its two dictionaries, in one statement: Jazz on top at 6.57.

sort_values returns a new table and leaves the old one alone, the opposite of list.sort(). To keep a result, give it a name.

Same idea, three notations: the session 4 counting dictionary, session 5's GROUP BY genre, and value_counts() give identical numbers (Electronic 547, Folk 438). Notebook 01 has them side by side.

No HAVING keyword is the quiet win here. In SQL, WHERE versus HAVING exists because the database needs telling when a filter happens. In pandas the order of the lines is the order things happen, so the question never comes up.

The ValueError is a good error

pandas guessed the format from the first row, met a row that did not fit, and stopped. Loud and honest.

format="mixed" gets 159 dates wrong

It reads 03/09/2025 month first, the American way. It only gets 16/08/2024 right because there is no 16th month.

430 European dates. 174 have a day of 12 or less, so they fit either way round. 15 of those have the same day and month. 174 − 15 = 159.

Do the arithmetic with them. It is the same habit as the fan-out row count an hour ago: do not trust a number until you can explain it from smaller numbers.

The error message itself suggests format="mixed". That is worth pointing out: following a helpful suggestion is exactly how silent bugs get in.

Duplicates move totals a lot and rates hardly at all. The copies added 161.9 minutes, and the skip rate only went from 11.9% to 11.8%. Which one you are reporting decides how much this step matters.

Fix 7, the judgement call

Three ways to handle a gap, three answers

pandas skips NaN when it adds up or averages, which is why leaving and dropping agree.

fillna(0) is a claim

It says 94 plays lasted no time at all. That is a different, false statement, not a neutral placeholder.

dropna() with no arguments is a blunt tool

Any gap in any column drops the row: 159 plays, including 65 with perfectly good minutes that only lacked a device.

Recover, label, or leave

Genre and country were recovered. Device gets a label of its own, "unknown". Minutes stay NaN, which is the number for "nobody knows".

Part three

Dates and shapes

Two things every chart in session 7 needs: real dates, and a table in the right shape.

Text that looks like a date is not a date

.dt, and a total for every month

Text has .str, dates have .dt

The year, the month, the day of the week, for every row at once. The format is named on purpose, as in the cleaning.

groupby or resample

resample("MS") makes a row for every month in the range, even an empty one. That is a time series: what a line chart draws.

The sum of nothing is 0

May 2024 on the tablet: one play, minutes unknown. The mean says NaN, the sum says 0.0. sum(min_count=1) keeps it unknown.

Keep this quick; the notebook has the detail. The busiest month is October 2024 with 127 plays, the quietest August 2025 with 57, and June, July and August are the three quietest months for minutes in both years.

The sum of nothing is the zero count from twenty minutes ago, in a new costume: a fillna(0) nobody asked for. A line chart of it would dip to the floor in May and say the tablet was not used.

The shape a chart wants

Long to wide: unstack and pivot_table

Long is one row per pair, good for storing. Wide is one row per month and one column per device: one line per column on a chart.

The genre table is the session 4 stretch, built by hand there, one line here. fill_value=0 is right for counts: no rows means no plays.

The session 4 stretch promised this table in one line of pandas. Folk fell most, by 34 plays; only Jazz and Afrobeat grew.

The question to ask about every gap, again: does it mean none, or unknown? Plays per month per artist has 275 empty cells, and they really are zero plays, so fill_value=0 is the truth there. Minutes that nobody wrote down are not.
